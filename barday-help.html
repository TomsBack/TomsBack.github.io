Help

How BarDay sets your goal, runs your training programs and tells you if your pace is enough.

Setup

Enter your daily PR (the most pull-ups you have done in one day), your attempt window in hours (1 to 24), and a long-term ceiling goal. You can change all three later in Profile.

Where the daily goal comes from

When you tap Start Attempt, BarDay sets a goal that is a small step above your best day, rounded up to a multiple of 5 and never above your ceiling. The step is smaller as your numbers grow, so recovery keeps up. Profile shows the next generated target.

Logging sets

On Today, tap +1, +5 or +10 to add reps at once. Tap Add Set to pick the exact reps, see your rest time, and see what your pace will be after the set.

Pace

current pace  = reps so far / hours elapsed
required pace = (goal − reps so far) / hours left in your window
finish        = current pace × your window in hours

The Pace screen compares the two paces and shows the total you are on track to reach. If it is below your goal, it shows how many reps you are short. It also gives a target for the next hour. In the first 15 minutes, current pace is worked out as if 15 minutes had passed.

Undoing a set

Tap Undo on Today to remove the last set you logged. Tap it again to remove the one before. BarDay does not edit older sets, so undo back to the set you want to change and log it again.

Finishing the day

Tap Finish Day to end the attempt. The Result screen shows one of four verdicts:

  • Finished: you ended below your goal.
  • Goal Met: you reached 100% of your goal.
  • Goal Buried: you reached 110% of your goal or more.
  • New Daily PR: you beat your previous daily PR.

What PR means

PR is your personal record. Your daily PR is the most pull-ups you have logged in one day. It rises when a finished day beats it, and the next goal rises with it.

History

On the Pace tab, switch to History. It shows each day's total, duration, number of sets, best hour, average rest, hourly volume and every set with its time.

Exercises

Pick an exercise on Today: pull-ups, chin-ups, dips, push-ups, hanging leg raises, muscle-ups or dead hang. Each exercise has its own daily goal, history and record. Dead hang is counted in seconds.

Training programs

The Programs tab lists five programs: First Pull-up, 10 Pull-ups, Ladder, Pyramid and Grease the Groove. Tap one, then Start Program. Today shows today's session. Finish every set and the next session gets harder. Fall short and it gets easier. Tap Stop Program to end it. Your logged sets stay in your history.

Rest timer

On Today, tap Start Rest. Each program has its own rest time, and you can add 15 seconds or skip. The timer buzzes when it ends. If BarDay is in the background and notifications are allowed, it sends a notification. You can start the timer after each set automatically.

Timed hold

On Today, tap Timed Hold for a stopwatch with a countdown. Use it for dead hangs and top holds (chin over the bar). Tap Save Hold to add a dead hang to your log. A hold must last at least 1 second.

Progress and badges

The Progress tab shows your records (most in one set, longest hold, best day, best week, total), charts by day, week and month, and a calendar of your streak. Tap the medal to see all 15 badges. Each badge shows how far you are from earning it.

Widget

Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap the add button and choose BarDay. The widget shows today's pull-ups toward your goal and your streak.

Apple Health

In Profile, turn on Save workouts to Health. Each day you finish from then on is saved as a strength training workout with your training time and sets. BarDay reads nothing from Health. If saving fails, allow BarDay in Settings under Privacy & Security, then Health.

Starting over

Reset app at the bottom of Profile erases your setup and all history, then returns you to setup. This cannot be undone. Changing your daily PR, hours or ceiling in Profile does not delete anything.

Your data

Everything is stored on your phone. BarDay has no account, no ads and no purchases, and it does not send your data anywhere. There is no sync, so a new phone starts empty.
